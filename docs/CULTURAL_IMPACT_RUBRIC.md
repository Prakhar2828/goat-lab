# Cultural Impact Rubric

## Purpose

Cultural impact is evaluated separately from basketball performance.

The category combines:

- 20% common-window Wikimedia attention
- 80% sourced historical and institutional rubric

Wikimedia begins in July 2015 and therefore cannot represent complete
career-period attention, particularly for Michael Jordan.

## Dimension weights

- Commercial and global reach: 30%
- Basketball-culture influence: 30%
- Media and entertainment reach: 15%
- Philanthropy and social institutions: 25%

## Anchor scale

- 90-100: category-defining global, multidecade, or institutional impact
- 80-89: sustained international or multi-institution impact
- 70-79: sustained national impact
- 50-69: meaningful but narrower impact
- Below 50: limited documented evidence

## Scoring principles

1. Every score must reference source IDs from `data/manual/sources.csv`.
2. Financial values must come from the company or recipient institution.
3. Audience claims must come from the distributor or measurement release.
4. Qualitative influence claims receive lower confidence unless supported
   by an institution, league, or directly measurable proxy.
5. Attention spikes are not treated as complete historical equivalence.
6. Scores remain editable judgments rather than claims of objective truth.
7. Missing dimensions block the final cultural-impact score.

## Current interpretation

Michael Jordan receives the stronger commercial and basketball-culture
scores because of the scale and durability of Jordan Brand and the NBA's
documented assessment of his role in basketball globalization.

LeBron James receives the stronger media-ownership and social-institution
scores because of SpringHill's athlete-led media ecosystem and the breadth
of the I PROMISE community infrastructure.
